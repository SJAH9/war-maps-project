# The War Maps Project: First Public Release

**Release:** v0.3.0  
**Published:** 2026-08-29  
**Status:** Public research and engineering release

## Project horizon

The War Maps Project will produce navigable nested causal models of every organized conflict for which suitable peer-reviewed datasets are available. The atlas is designed to move from a reproducible observed field into recursively addressed causal depth without collapsing source records, claims, uncertainty, or plausible branches into a single authorized narrative.

Peer review is an eligibility and provenance boundary for expansion, not a truth flag. Every imported dataset retains its version, publisher, methods, coverage, citation, license, identifiers, and known limitations. Every observation remains enclosed by those conditions.

## What this release establishes

The first public release provides:

- the complete UCDP/PRIO state-based conflict-year register from 1946 through 2025;
- 11,867 unique UCDP Candidate observations through July 2026, reconciled by stable event ID;
- 485 navigable candidate conflict clusters across state-based, non-state, and one-sided violence;
- V-Dem country-year conditions and Regimes of the World classifications;
- world, conflict, and nation exploration with Date, All time, and Raw data views;
- searchable war selection, time windows, government-type overlays, event maps, and relationship globes;
- brief, standard, and archive print volumes generated from the same canonical data;
- machine-readable generated data and retained official raw source files; and
- automated tests for coverage, joins, event uniqueness, nation geometry, enclosure form, and attribution boundaries.

The optional satellite layer is cartographic context. It keeps public orbital geometry separate from a documented constellation-level relationship and does not infer individual satellite tasking.

## What this release does not claim

This release does not claim that every listed conflict already contains a complete nested causal model. The historical register establishes an addressable outer field. Causal depth depends on the observations and peer-reviewed datasets available for a conflict and on the recursive questions opened at each frontier.

Candidate events remain provisional within the UCDP Candidate enclosure. Same-side and opposing-state relationships describe coded conflict participation, not permanent alliances or intrinsic national identities. Plausible alternatives and alleged backchannels remain distinct projection branches rather than observed chronology.

## Expansion path

Future releases will:

1. identify additional peer-reviewed conflict, actor, casualty, economic, institutional, agreement, and event datasets;
2. preserve each dataset's native identifiers and methodological enclosure;
3. join observations through explicit, repeatable keys rather than narrative similarity alone;
4. deepen individual conflict records from outer conditions through decisions and actors to enclosed consequences;
5. retain unresolved and alternative branches at stable addresses; and
6. generate web, machine-readable, and print representations from the same canonical model.

Expansion is governed by source availability and causal addressability. Missing evidence remains missing, and an open frontier remains visible until another properly enclosed observation opens it.
