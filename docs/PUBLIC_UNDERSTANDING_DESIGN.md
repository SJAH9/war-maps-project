# Public-understanding interface

The War Maps interface is designed for a person who has encountered a conflict in the news and wants to understand the record around it. It is not designed to provide strategic advantage, targeting information, operational advice, or advocacy for any party.

## Information architecture

The start page asks questions before naming visualization types:

1. Where and when was conflict recorded?
2. Who appears in a selected conflict?
3. How are parties connected in the loaded evidence?
4. What do the civilian-death records show?
5. What wider living conditions surround the record?
6. How do source-dated fuel prices differ?

Primary navigation then uses stable subject labels: Start, Conflict network, World map, Relationships, Human cost, Living conditions, Fuel prices, and Evidence guide. Specialist controls remain inside the view that uses them.

## Evidence states

Every view must keep four states distinguishable in text as well as color:

- **Source record:** a value or relationship contained in a named upstream dataset.
- **Project transformation:** a join, total, conversion, layout, or scale generated by this project.
- **Inference:** an explicitly labelled interpretation. It is not promoted to a source fact or legal conclusion.
- **Unknown or missing:** a gap that remains unfilled. No record is not evidence of no event.

The shared reading panel above each visualization states the question the view can answer, explains its marks in plain language, identifies its evidence status, and links directly to sources, method, and coverage.

## Visual and interaction rules

- Begin with a plain-language question and a one-paragraph reading instruction.
- Put source links beside the view that uses them, not only on a remote citations page.
- State the unit, time boundary, geographic resolution, and denominator near the value.
- Use a fixed global scale when the task is comparison across filters; disclose clipping or normalization.
- Never rely on color alone. Pair it with labels, shapes, positions, patterns, or inspectable values.
- Preserve a text route through interactive graphics with inspectors, registers, lists, tables, or downloads.
- Use visible keyboard focus, reflow narrow screens, and honor reduced-motion preferences.
- Keep at most seven or eight top-level navigation choices; group specialist actions inside each view.
- Prefer familiar language in navigation. Define project-specific and source-specific terms at first use.
- Do not imply causation, intent, responsibility, legitimacy, or moral equivalence from proximity, size, map position, or correlation.

## Privacy and delivery

The static atlas has no analytics, ad technology, user profiles, cookies, or server-side interaction. Browser local storage is used only to remember the reader's chosen light or dark theme. Third-party scripts provide rendering libraries; data interpretation remains local to the published page.

## Review checklist

Before publication, verify for every data-bearing page:

- the reader can tell what question the view answers;
- every visible metric has a unit and temporal boundary;
- a direct source or source-guide link is adjacent to the visualization;
- project calculations are identified as transformations or inferences;
- missingness and exclusions are stated without euphemism;
- the same information has a usable nonvisual path;
- controls remain keyboard reachable and readable at narrow widths;
- the page makes no claim beyond what its loaded evidence supports.
