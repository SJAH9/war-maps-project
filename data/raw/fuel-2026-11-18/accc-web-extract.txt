2                                     ACCC Ι Weekly fuel price monitoring report Ι Friday 13 November 2026
    Key messages
In March 2026, the ACCC increased its reporting of the fuel industry from quarterly to weekly
reports in response to the conflict in the Middle East and flow on impacts to Australia.1
This is our thirty-sixth weekly report, with prices to 11 November.
The report provides the latest movements in crude oil, international refined fuel benchmarks, and
domestic wholesale and retail fuel prices around Australia. A summary of other fuel-related ACCC
actions is available on our website.


Price movements over the last week
● Australian retail fuel prices are largely determined by movements in international refined fuel
    benchmark prices, which have been relatively high. Over the last week, average international
    refined petrol and diesel prices decreased.
● Retail petrol² prices decreased across all capital cities.

    ○ On 11 November, across the 5 largest cities (Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Adelaide and
       Perth), daily average retail petrol prices were 219.2 cpl, a decrease of 13.5 cpl (or around 6%)
       from the previous week.
    ○ Compared with 20 February, around a week before the conflict between the United States,
       Israel and Iran escalated, average retail petrol prices across the 5 largest cities were 48.3 cpl
       higher on 11 November.
    ○ Compared with 31 March, when retail prices were around the highest following the escalation
       of the Middle Eastern conflict, average retail petrol prices across the 5 largest cities were
       38.0 cpl lower on 11 November.
● Retail diesel prices decreased across all capital cities.

    ○ On 11 November, daily average retail diesel prices across the 5 largest cities were 266.4 cpl,
       a decrease of 9.4 cpl (or around 3%) from the previous week.
    ○ Compared with 20 February, average retail diesel prices across the 5 largest cities were
       89.8 cpl higher on 11 November. Compared with 31 March, average retail diesel prices
       across the 5 largest cities were 56.0 cpl lower on 11 November.
●   Across regional locations average retail petrol and diesel prices decreased.

    ○ Across more than 190 ACCC monitored regional locations, daily average retail prices (in
       aggregate) on 11 November for petrol were 236.9 cpl (a decrease of 7.9 cpl or around 3%
       from the previous week), and for diesel they were 276.0 cpl (a decrease of 6.5 cpl or around
       2% from the previous week).

We encourage consumers to shop around by using fuel price apps and
websites to compare retail prices at different retail sites before filling up.

International refined petrol and diesel prices
Australian retail fuel prices are largely determined by movements in international refined fuel
benchmark prices (which are driven by international crude oil prices), and the AUD-USD exchange
rate. International benchmark price movements drive domestic wholesale prices, which then
influence retail fuel prices.

Additionally, in the 5 largest cities (Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Adelaide and Perth), retail petrol
prices have typically moved up and down in regular patterns, known as petrol price cycles. The
ACCC petrol price cycles web page has more information on these price cycles:
https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/petrol-and-fuel/petrol-price-cycles-in-the-5-largest-cities.
Retail diesel prices do not move in cycles.


On 20 February, average wholesale (terminal gate) petrol and diesel prices started edging up from
the relatively stable levels that had been observed since the start of 2026.

On 28 February, conflict escalated between the United States, Israel and Iran.

International benchmark prices for refined petrol and diesel are the largest component of retail
petrol and diesel prices.

The international benchmark for Australian retail petrol prices is Singapore Mogas 95 (Mogas 95).
The international benchmark for Australian retail diesel prices is Singapore Gasoil with 10 parts per
million sulphur content (Gasoil 10 ppm).

Both petrol and diesel are refined from crude oil and their prices generally tend to follow similar
movements over the long term. However, the different fuels have their own supply and demand
characteristics (for example, compared with petrol, diesel also has remote energy generation and
industrial uses) and the different refined fuels have their own international benchmark prices. It is
these benchmarks that drive retail petrol and diesel prices.

Petrol prices in capital cities

Terminal gate prices are prices that wholesalers charge for petrol and diesel in the spot market. The
major wholesalers post these prices on their websites on a regular basis. Although few wholesale
transactions occur at terminal gate prices and actual costs can vary across brands and cities, they
are typically indicative of average wholesale prices. Terminal gate prices are indicative of the
wholesale price of petrol or diesel only, and exclude transport and other retail operating costs.

Note on terminal gate prices in the current environment

The ACCC has used terminal gate prices as a transparent and timely proxy for general movements in
wholesale prices over time. The Australian Institute of Petroleum (AIP) issued a statement on
11 March about fuel supply in Australia , which noted that AIP members had been hit by a sudden
rush of demand for fuel, and members had to prioritise supply to their regular customers and cease
spot sales. Given this, it is likely there have been fewer than normal transactions at or near terminal
gate prices since the supply disruptions. The ACCC also heard from market participants that some
transactions have been at quite different prices to published terminal gate prices.

International, wholesale and retail price movements
The following figures illustrate movements in average international refined petrol (Mogas 95) and
diesel (Gasoil 10 ppm) benchmark prices, daily terminal gate prices and retail prices for petrol
and diesel in each capital city from 1 January to 11 November 2026.

For each city, the figures show average retail prices on each day (the purple lines for petrol, and
green lines for diesel) and average terminal gate prices on the same day (the orange lines).

The figures also illustrate average terminal gate prices lagged by 7 days. Typically, there are lags
between changes in wholesale prices and retail prices, because it is generally only when fuel
stocks are replenished at a retail site that changes in wholesale prices flow through to retail
prices. The ACCC has previously noted the lag in the larger cities is generally between 1 and 2
weeks.
The figures show trends in the difference between average terminal gate prices and average retail
prices. The ACCC has generally considered this difference over time to be a broad indicator of
average gross retail margins (including both retail operating costs and profits). Prices, costs and
margins can vary significantly between retailers.
