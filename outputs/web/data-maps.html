Source guide

Gas and civilian-casualty map data

The Gas Map and Civilian Casualties map are source-dated views with different measurements. Fuel prices are a one-date market snapshot. Civilian casualties are event records selected by an inclusive year range. Neither layer is a live feed or a complete census.

Every price is converted for display to indicative U.S. dollars per U.S. gallon. Tower height uses one fixed global linear scale from $0 to a $10-per-gallon ceiling: $5 is exactly half the height of $10 in every region and fuel selection. Values above $10 meet the ceiling without changing the scale, while their exact values remain in the inspector and payload. The payload also retains original currency and unit. Most exchange rates use the European Central Bank reference rates; Taiwan uses the Central Bank of the Republic of China rate.

Civilian Casualties sources

The primary layer uses UCDP Georeferenced Event Dataset 26.1 deaths_civilians values for 1989–2025 and the loaded UCDP candidate-event releases through September 2026. Values are summed by the country where UCDP locates the event and only within the selected inclusive year range. The default range is the latest ten calendar years, currently 2017–2026. Independent toggles place IHME GBD 2023 all-cause mortality and long-run life expectancy beside casualties. The latter cites Riley's peer-reviewed historical estimates, HMD, and UN WPP 2024. Health layers use only actual observations inside the selected interval and never extrapolate them.

Gaza and West Bank context records from the oPt Health Cluster / WHO Unified Health Dashboard remain outside UCDP and are never added to its world total. The Gaza dashboard observation replaces the overlapping older OCHA/MoH snapshot, whose provenance remains attached. AOAV's separate explosive-violence record is retained. These sources have different measurement boundaries and cannot be added or treated as equivalent civilian-only estimates. Injury counts are shown separately from deaths.

Download the frozen dashboard aggregates and injury-aware graph data: casualty reporting sequences, demographic tables and health-attack location links. Graph nodes retain deaths and injuries separately. Health-attack losses overlap area totals; cumulative daily rows must never be summed. The period-share demographic table contains fractions, not counts. No patient, contact or mailing-list records are ingested. Source URL, dates, query provenance and publisher terms accompany the data.

Reference geometry

Natural Earth supplies country outlines. U.S. state and Japanese prefecture reference geometry and GeoNames location coordinates support subnational placement. Geometry is a display reference and does not adjudicate borders.

Neutrality · Purpose · Data integrity

The War Maps Project is an open information design project working with open data. It is not an intelligence product or a tactical data source. It is neutral to every conflict represented in the data.

The War Maps Project honors the dead by making them visible to the world. A human life has the same value regardless of the side, nationality, or civilian or military status recorded in the source. Showing a conflict, actor, border, or loss does not express allegiance or endorsement.

Our work is to make public records readable through maps, diagrams, print publications, and art. Source attribution, definitions, dates, and distinctions between recorded data and our visual interpretation belong with that work. Readers can examine the methods and obtain the underlying data directly from its publishers.

Sources and their published standards

Uppsala Conflict Data Program (UCDP): The conflict and event datasets use published definitions and systematic coding. UCDP describes evaluating sources for independence and transparency, tracing reports to their origins, and assessing reliability in context. Its materials include news reporting, international and nongovernmental organizations, research, historical archives, and truth commission reports. These are research datasets, not a collection of headlines turned into points. UCDP methodology · Data and codebooks.

V-Dem: The governance data comes with a published measurement methodology. V-Dem combines expert assessments using a statistical model that accounts for differences in coding and reports estimates with uncertainty intervals. V-Dem methodology.

WHO: WHO’s data principles call for accessible data, transparent methods, provenance and metadata, and adherence to scientific data standards. The oPt Health Cluster dashboard identifies the Palestinian Ministry of Health as the source of its casualty series; that attribution remains part of our presentation. WHO data principles.

CelesTrak: Public orbital elements are fitted from observations for orbit estimation. The satellite layer presents dated estimates from saved elements, not live tracking or operational tasking. CelesTrak GP documentation.

These are the publishers’ documented methods and standards, not a claim that every source makes the same neutrality declaration. The source register identifies the other geographic, health, population, economic, and organizational sources, with attribution and release information. Our neutrality statement belongs to War Maps; it does not speak on behalf of those publishers.
